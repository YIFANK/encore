## CONFOUNDS — read before interpreting the table

**1. The reference cells had an EMPTY law library.** All three are c2 wave-1
cells (c2/STATE.md R1, 2026-08-14): `mkworker.sh` copies `LAWS.md` at
workspace-creation time and c2's library started empty. So the reference
condition is *mined pack + empty library + law-writing discipline +
probe/verify loop*, and the abl_c2 baseline is pinned to match it (variants A
and C get an empty `LAWS.md`; the campaign `LAWS.md` here is empty by design).

Consequence for **variant B**: B cannot measure the *seeded-law* channel,
because the reference received no seeded laws either. What B removes that the
reference had is the **law-writing discipline** — the requirement to distil
observations into named, falsifiable, receipt-backed cross-episode
regularities. That is a real within-cell method component (it forces the
worker to state a mechanism before changing code), and it is what B's number
measures. Any paper claim about a *shared law library* needs a separate arm:
the same three tasks run with c2's final `LAWS.md` seeded. That arm is NOT in
this campaign's nine cells; it is flagged here as the natural follow-up.

**2. Variant A is a presentation ablation, not an information-removal one.**
`raw_demos.json` is a strict superset of `pack.json`'s numeric content: dense
per-timestep proprio and dense actions vs the pack's 3-5 mined keyframes,
strided `ee_path6` and `action_scale`. A therefore tests whether the
coordinator-side distillation helps the agent *use* the demonstration, with
information held constant-or-greater. It is NOT the LADDER's `keyframes`/`text`
rungs, which remove information. Frame counts: A gets 27/28/44 uniform-stride
RGB frames vs the mined packs' 9/13/10 keyframes, so A is not starved of
images either.

**3. The version budget differs from the reference on one of the three tasks.**
The task contract caps A/B at 5 program versions. Counted from the c2 result
dirs on AbakaAI, the reference cells used:

| task | c2 versions | c2 learn-phase episodes (debug seeds, all probes + selections) |
|---|---|---|
| turn on the stove | 3 (+ a v1_1 and a v3t re-probe) | **60** |
| put the bowl on the plate | 4 (+ a v1_1) | **64** |
| open the middle drawer | 11 (v1..v11, + a v111 re-selection) | **86** |

So the 5-version cap is **non-binding** for stove and bowl_on_plate (the
reference itself converged in 3-4), and **binding** for the drawer, where the
reference needed 11. A depressed A or B number on the drawer row is therefore
partly a budget effect and must not be read as a pure component effect. Stove
and bowl_on_plate rows carry no such contamination. Learn-phase episode counts
above are the reference points for the sample-efficiency curve.

**4. Variant C's failure modes include harness paperwork.** A no-verify worker
can emit a program the fair gate statically refuses (forbidden token, missing
PROVENANCE, `api.done` read), which would produce *no eval* rather than a
score. The briefs therefore permit C workers a purely-static self-check
(`py_compile` + the gate's own token/AST/PROVENANCE rules) so that C's number
reflects policy quality rather than paperwork. Zero episodes remain
forbidden. This is a deliberate concession recorded here.

### R8 — 2026-08-20 (the first discriminating cell: no-verify collapses on the drawer)

**`eval_ablC_goal_open_middle_drawer` = 19/50** against the full-ENCORE
reference's **48/50**. First cell in the campaign that separates a variant
from the reference.

Post-hoc mechanism, read from the frozen cell's own `api.log` lines (the cell
is frozen; this is forensics for the ledger, not iteration). Three failure
modes, and **all three are items the worker itself listed, in advance, as
what it would have probed first**:

1. **The closed-loop correction never fired, on any episode.** Every log
   opens with `cabinet mask implausible (1184 px); no shift` — the
   plausibility gate the worker built to make perception fail safe rejected
   its own detector on all 50 layouts, because the mask statistics were
   calibrated on 128x128 keyframes and the runtime view is 512x512. The
   policy therefore degraded to pure replay for the whole eval. This is
   verbatim the worker's probe item #3 ("whether `cam_high` at 512x512 is the
   same view, orientation and crop as the 128x128 keyframes — my whole
   perception layer rests on that; a single capture would have settled it").
2. **The hook height was wrong and the retry ladder was the only thing
   scoring.** Attempt 1 (`dz = 0`) essentially never catches the handle;
   attempt 2 (`dz = -0.014`) is what produces the 19 successes; attempt 3
   (`dz = +0.014`) almost never recovers. Probe item #1 was "hook height z —
   the single biggest unknown; a 3-point z sweep (+/-10 mm) on one debug seat
   would have collapsed this immediately. It is the reason the retry loop
   exists at all." The retry loop was a hedge against an unknown that one
   probe episode would have removed.
3. **The self-imposed time budget then ran out**: failures end on
   `finished (budget 0.0 s left)` after the third attempt, with the open-band
   sensor reading 361-371 px against a closed baseline of ~230 and an open
   signature of ~850 — i.e. the drawer was being dragged *partly* open and the
   episode ended before the predicate could fire. Probe item #5 was
   "`api.move` timing semantics — how `seconds` maps to control steps, and the
   episode horizon."

**Why this is the campaign's most useful cell so far.** It separates two
things that are easy to conflate. The no-verify worker's *reasoning* was not
worse than the reference's — it correctly identified the mechanism (open
gripper, hook the handle in the finger crook, drag +y), correctly derived the
insertion point to sub-millimetre cross-demo agreement in y, and correctly
enumerated its own load-bearing unknowns. What it could not do is resolve any
of them. The verify loop's contribution here is not insight; it is
**measurement** — converting three named unknowns into three numbers. That
is a sharper claim than "iteration helps", and it is supported by a
prediction the worker made *before* seeing any outcome.

Caveat, stated plainly: the drawer row is also the row where the 5-version
cap binds (reference used 11), but that caveat does NOT apply to this cell —
variant C is capped at one version by its own definition, not by the campaign
budget, and its comparison against the 48/50 reference is clean.

### R11 — 2026-08-20 (second no-verify collapse — and it is the SAME root cause)

**`eval_ablC_goal_bowl_on_plate` = 16/50** against the reference's 50/50.

Forensics from the frozen cell's own log lines, aggregated over all 50
episodes:

    50/50 episodes | "bowl not segmented -> pack fallback"
    50/50 episodes | "plate not segmented -> pack fallback"

**The perception layer never fired once.** Not on a single layout. The worker
built a 45-constant segmenter — the plate's salmon rim ring (R-G~35, G-B~6)
anchoring a uniform bright interior (value 168, std 5), the bowl as the
compact mottled neutral blob (value ~110, std 33-38) — every statistic
measured off the pack's 128x128 keyframes, and none of it transferred to the
512x512 runtime view. Its own plausibility gate then did what it was designed
to do and routed to the pack fallback constants, i.e. a fixed pose. That
scores whenever the sampled layout happens to sit near the demo layout: 16/50.
Successes are the episodes where grasp try 1 held; failures burned up to three
tries without a hold ("no hold after try N" x88).

**This is the same failure as R8, to the letter.** The drawer cell's log read
`cabinet mask implausible (1184 px); no shift` on 50/50 episodes; the bowl
cell's reads `not segmented -> pack fallback` on 50/50 episodes. Two
independent workers, two different tasks, two independently-designed
perception stacks — and one identical root cause: **image statistics
calibrated on the pack's 128x128 keyframes do not transfer to the 512x512
runtime camera, and a program that never runs an episode cannot discover
this.** In both cells the worker's defensive gating worked exactly as
intended and converted a silent wrong answer into a silent fallback, which is
better engineering and the same score.

Both workers named this as a probe they wanted. The bowl worker's list opened
with the jaw-closing axis and put "whether the appliance blob ever beats the
bowl in the scorer **at 512x512**" last; the drawer worker's item #3 was
"whether `cam_high` at 512x512 is the same view, orientation and crop as the
128x128 keyframes — my whole perception layer rests on that. A single capture
would have settled it."

**CANDIDATE LAW (campaign-level, falsifiable) — ABL-L1.** *A demonstration
pack fixes a policy's geometry but not its perception: any constant read off
pack imagery is uncalibrated against the runtime camera, and one capture is
worth more than any amount of offline reasoning about it.* Receipt: two
independent no-verify cells, different tasks, different detectors, both with
0/50 perception-fire rate and both degrading to pack-constant replay
(19/50 and 16/50 vs 48/50 and 50/50 references). Prediction: a no-verify arm
allowed exactly ONE episode — enough for a single `api.capture` — recovers
most of the gap on both cells, while a no-verify arm given more offline
reasoning time recovers none of it. That is a cheap, decisive follow-up and
the single most valuable experiment this campaign has surfaced.

**The A-vs-B divergence on this row is the campaign's sharpest open question.**
Same task, same empty-library condition, same 5-version cap: variant A
(raw dump) reached 15/15 in 3 versions and 21 episodes; variant B (mined pack)
reached 0/15 in 5 versions and 43 episodes. The two workers found different
mechanisms. A read the DENSE ACTION STREAM and found that the demo drags under
a saturated downward command (`dz ~ -0.94` while measured z stays flat), then
replayed that *load* rather than the poses. B anchored on the pack's keyframes,
